01

Casa La Scala

Research / Analysis

Analysis of a brutalist house — Vittoriano Viganò.

A group project in which we examined the brutalist house on Lake Garda by Vittoriano Viganò. My group member and I focused on the modular furniture and the use of colour. The different sofas could easily be moved and stacked, creating endless possibilities for different compositions and layouts of the space. The series is called “Cavalletto,” designed in 1956 by Angelo Mangiarotti and Bruno Morassutti.

In collaboration with Heleen Van Acker

02

The Edge of Living

Studio Project

Only a sailor's knot as a connection to society — architect of your own life.

The cargo ship is a continuously changing object and, in itself, essentially a permanent worksite. Steel plates are welded on where necessary to ensure its quality. It can be compared to a human being — something that is never complete and always leaves room for improvement; something that allows bad traits to be hidden behind a new, good one. I believe that we, as humans, are constantly evolving: every new encounter, experience, job and emotion contributes to who we are. As long as we live, we experience and continue to take shape each day. The human as a worksite, the worksite as a cargo ship, the cargo ship as a human. Because I have learned so much from visiting boats, I decided to showcase living on a boat. Just like Luc Deleu with his floating U.I.A., I introduce an educational aspect to my design: visitors can temporarily stay on the boat or take part in ongoing activities. By incorporating public restrooms and installations, I want to welcome people aboard. The boat has a kitchen, two restrooms, a bathroom, a bedroom and an outdoor space, just like a traditional home.

04

Western Cemetery Portal

Public / Social

A manifesto to make the portal of the Western Cemetery part of the public space — breaking the taboo surrounding cemeteries through architecture.

The cemetery is increasingly seen and used as a park, where people come to walk, cycle and picnic. It features a large entrance portal, only a small part of which is currently used for reception and administrative staff. The receptionist told me the main entrance will be relocated to Leliestraat to make part of the cemetery car-free; just behind the portal lies a green open space where children can play and cycle. For safety reasons, the entrance is being moved.

As a result, the portal will be left vacant. I propose a new use for it that addresses the needs of residents, creating space for social cohesion in the neighbourhood. The active social organisations already in the area point to a very engaged network, and my aim is to support it from a spatial perspective. The design focuses on the entrance portal and the driveway leading up to it, within an environment that honours and respects the various emotions present on the site — so that people with different motivations can express their wishes and feelings without hindering, or feeling hindered by, one another.

The left side of the portal is set up as a toy library — a place for lending toys. Many young children in the neighbourhood grow up in families with limited means, and by lending rather than selling, an affordable alternative can be offered. The toy library also includes an orangery, a space where children can play with the toys before taking them home.
